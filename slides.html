01 · The Premise

A kid, a town, and one big dream

You play a child in a small, friendly town whose biggest wish is to adopt a puppy. Puppies aren't free — adoption fees, food, toys, and supplies all cost money. So you work, save, and wait, and when the day finally comes, the story doesn't end at adoption. It begins.

The real goal: not just getting a dog — building a happy little life together.

02 · Why We're Building This

A pause button, disguised as a game

Paws & Pause exists for the twenty minutes after work or after school — when you want to unwind, not compete. There's a whole small world here: your own room, your own look, and a pet who's happy to just see you.

No fail states, no leaderboards, nobody to lose to. Just a gentle, heartwarming place that's built for everyone — every gender, every age.

05 · Feature

Part-time jobs — starting at the café

The signature job is the café kitchen: orders come in as a short recipe ticket, and you tap the right stations — bread, veggie, grill, plate — in the right order before time's up. Overcooked-sized, cozy-paced.

  • Café cooking — read the ticket, hit the stations in order, earn tips
  • More jobs to come — delivery, gardening, pet sitting as the town grows
06 · Feature

Puppy adoption

The long-term goal the whole loop points toward. Before the savings goal is hit, players can visit the shelter — see the puppies waiting, meet them through the window — turning the countdown into anticipation, not just a locked shop item. Then you pick your actual dog, by breed, from a real lineup at the shelter.

After adoption, spending shifts from "saving up" to "keeping up": food, toys, beds, clothes, accessories, decorations.

07 · Feature

Puppy care, bonding & feelings

Daily interactions

Feed, walk, play, and pet — the rituals of caring for a dog, done right in your own home.

Two-stat relationship

Happiness rises and gently decays day to day. Bond only ever grows — one bad day never erases the friendship.

Real feelings

Your puppy's mood — from ecstatic to sad — shows on its face, right there on the map. Care for it, and you can see it work.

08 · Feature

Make them really yours

A proper, Mii-style character creator: skin tone, eye shape and color, mouth, hairstyle, and clothing silhouette — mix and match freely, no two kids need to look alike. The puppy gets the same care, with its own coat, breed, and collar color.

It's the same philosophy on both ends of the leash — child and dog grow their look together, one matching pair building a life side by side.

09 · Feature

Room decoration

A major, purely positive progression system — decorate the child's bedroom and the puppy's own space using everything earned from jobs and mini-games.

Beds & rugs Wallpaper Shelves & lamps Dog beds & houses Food bowls Toy storage
10 · Feature

The energy system

Every activity — working, walking, playing, exploring, caring for the puppy — draws down an energy meter. Hit zero, and the child is too tired to keep working: it's time to head home and rest.

Sleep restores energy for the next day, giving the whole game its daily heartbeat and rewarding a bit of planning over rushing everything at once.

11 · Feature

Four ways to play

Fishing, at a real lake

Cast, wait for the bite, reel in — and collect Fish Cards for every species you catch.

Café cooking

Read the ticket, hit the right stations in order, beat the clock for better tips.

Go-Kart Stadium

A real steering-and-drifting kart race: 3 laps, 2 rivals, boost pads, podium payouts.

Arcade platformer

A pure, no-timer hop-and-collect level — coins, stompable critters, a flag at the end.

12 · Feature

A town that talks back

Villagers wander the town with their own faces and quiet routines. Walk up to one and start a conversation — a little wholesome small talk about the weather, the lake, or your dog's good manners. Nothing transactional, just company.

13 · Feature

A warm, lively town

HomeCafé LakeShelter Go-Kart StadiumArcade

Buildings read by shape alone — a cottage, a café awning, a stadium arch — no floating labels. A minimap in the corner and a handful of wandering neighbors make the whole place feel lived-in, not staged.
