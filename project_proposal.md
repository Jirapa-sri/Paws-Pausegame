# PAWS & PAUSE
## A Cozy Life Simulation Game
### Project Proposal

**Date:** August 3, 2026
*(Working title, formerly "Paw Prints")*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Paws & Pause is a cozy, single-player life simulation game about a child living in a small, friendly town whose biggest dream is to adopt and raise a puppy. The player earns money through a part-time job and an expanding set of mini-games, then adopts a puppy matched to them by AI — take a photo at the shelter, and the game pairs you with the dog that fits your personality — before building a daily life around caring for it, decorating a home, checking a morning board (fortune, island events, and an easy AI world-news digest), and getting to know the town.

Beyond the mechanics, the project has a specific purpose: it is designed as a short, low-stakes place to unwind — the twenty minutes after work or after school, with no fail states, no competition, and no one to lose to. The target experience is deliberately universal: heartwarming for every gender and every age, not aimed at a single demographic.

## 2. Core Story

The player controls a child living in a small, friendly town. Their biggest dream is to adopt and raise a puppy of their own — but puppies aren't free. Adoption fees, food, toys, and supplies all cost money, so the child works and saves. Once they've earned enough, they visit the shelter, take a photo, and AI matches them with the puppy that best suits their personality — not a random roll, but a personal pairing. The goal shifts from "getting a dog" to "building a happy life together."

The tone throughout is wholesome and low-stakes: no fail state, no combat, no punishing time pressure — only the gentle discipline of managing energy and money well enough to keep showing up for a dog who loves you back, in a town full of neighbors who are happy to see you too.

## 3. Target Audience

- Anyone who wants a short, relaxing wind-down after work or school
- Cozy-game fans
- Animal lovers and players drawn to pet-care fantasy
- Fans of Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing, and Little Kitty, Big City
- Explicitly designed to be welcoming across every gender and every age group, not a niche demographic

## 4. Core Gameplay Loop

Each in-game day follows the same warm, predictable rhythm:

1. Wake up with full energy
2. Check the morning board — AI fortuneteller, island news of the day, and an easy AI world-news digest
3. Explore the town (and today’s island events)
4. Complete a part-time job to earn money
5. Play a mini-game for additional income (from a growing roster)
6. Spend money on furniture, decorations, clothes, and eventually the puppy
7. Care for the puppy every day (post-adoption)
8. Decorate both the child's bedroom and the puppy's living space
9. Manage energy by resting when tired
10. Repeat — unlocking new content, new mini-games, and deepening the bond with the dog

## 5. Main Features

### 5.1 Part-Time Job: The Café
The player's steady income comes from working the café kitchen, styled after Overcooked and Cooking Mama: each order arrives as a short recipe ticket (for example, Bread → Veggie → Grill → Plate), and the player taps the matching stations in the correct order before time runs out. Faster, cleaner execution earns bigger tips. The sequence gets a little longer as a shift goes on, giving the job a light difficulty curve without ever punishing a wrong tap harshly.

### 5.2 Puppy Adoption — AI Photo Match
The central long-term goal is saving enough to adopt a puppy from the town shelter. Before the savings goal is hit, players can visit the shelter to see the puppies waiting, building anticipation rather than treating adoption as a locked shop item.

When ready to adopt, the player does **not** get a random dog and is not forced to pick blindly from a list. Instead they use the shelter **Photo Match** booth:

1. Take a short photobooth strip (or upload a selfie) of themselves
2. AI reads the player's vibe / personality cues from the photo
3. The system matches them with a shelter puppy whose breed and temperament suit that personality
4. The player sees why they were paired (a short match reason), can accept the match, or fall back to manual breed picking if they prefer
5. They name their new dog and bring it home

After adoption, spending continues on food, toys, beds, clothes, accessories, and decorations. The matched puppy also carries a personality trait — Playful, Shy, Energetic, or Sleepy — that gently shapes which care actions land best.

### 5.3 Puppy Care, Bonding & Feelings
Daily interactions: feeding, walking, playing, and petting. Two linked stats track the relationship: **Happiness** (short-term, rises with care and gently decays if neglected) and **Bond** (long-term, only ever increases). The puppy's personality trait comes from the AI photo match (with a clear, readable vibe) rather than a pure random roll — so the dog you bring home already feels like *your* match.

The puppy's current mood is visible directly, not just as a number: a small expressive face — from ecstatic to content to sad — shows on the puppy both in its care panel and out in the world, so the effect of daily care is something the player can actually see.

### 5.4 Character Customization
The child is customizable with a proper, Mii-style creator rather than a single fixed look: skin tone, eye shape and color, mouth expression, hairstyle, and clothing silhouette can all be mixed and matched independently, with colors and styles unlocked or freely re-picked at any time. The puppy receives the same care on a smaller scale — its own coat color (by breed) and a customizable collar/accessory color — so child and dog visibly grow their look together.

### 5.5 Room Decoration
Decorate the child's bedroom and the puppy's living space with wallpaper, rugs, furniture pieces, and dog beds. Decoration is a purely positive progression and self-expression system — there is no wrong way to decorate.

### 5.6 Energy System
Every activity drains energy — working, walking, running, playing mini-games, and caring for the puppy. At zero, the player must rest — creating the daily cycle and rewarding a bit of planning over rushing everything at once.

### 5.7 Mini-Games (Growing Roster)
Beyond the café job, the island keeps adding ways to play. The first four are **examples of the kinds of activities** players can expect — not a fixed, final list — and more mini-games will keep arriving over time:

- **Fishing at the lake** — cast, wait for a bite, and reel in at the right instant; every species caught is added to a collectible Fish Card album
- **Café cooking** (see 5.1) — also playable as a repeatable mini-game/job in its own right
- **Go-Kart Stadium** — a real steering-and-drifting kart race with acceleration, braking, and turning physics: three laps against two AI rivals, boost pads on the track, and a podium payout by finishing position
- **Arcade platformer** — a short, no-timer, side-scrolling hop-and-collect level: run, jump, stomp small critters, collect coins, and reach the flag at your own pace

New activities (seasonal booths, festival games, pet-focused challenges, and more) are planned as ongoing content so the town never feels like it “finished” after four modes.

### 5.8 Morning Board: Fortune, Island News & World Digest
Each morning, the player opens a small **morning board** at home (or at the island notice board). It has three gentle AI-powered sections:

**1. AI fortuneteller (daily tarot + weather)**  
Draw a tarot card for a short, cozy “today’s fortune” — a mood for the day rather than a high-stakes prophecy. That fortune is **linked to the island’s weather**:

- A bright, hopeful card may bring clear skies and warm light for walks
- A reflective or quiet card may bring soft clouds or a misty morning
- A restless or stormy card may bring rain — which can change outdoor plans (for example, the puppy may need a raincoat for walks)

**2. News of the day — island events**  
A friendly island bulletin lists **what’s happening on the island today**: café specials, lake fishing contests, Go-Kart exhibition races, arcade high-score nights, shelter open-house hours, festivals, visiting NPCs, and other small events. These tips help the player choose where to spend their day without feeling like a checklist of chores.

**3. Important world news — AI summary**  
A separate, calm panel brings in **important real-world news from around the world**. AI **summarizes** each story into a few plain sentences that are **easy to understand** for any age — no scare headlines, no political rabbit holes, no doom scroll. The tone stays soft and factual so players can stay lightly connected to the outside world while still feeling protected inside a cozy pause. Players can skip this panel entirely if they just want island life.

Weather, island events, and the world digest refresh each day so mornings feel alive without demanding a long read.

### 5.9 Town & NPCs
The town is designed to feel warm, welcoming, and lived-in. Buildings — Home, Café, Shelter, Fortuneteller / notice board, Go-Kart Stadium, and Arcade — are identified purely by their illustrated shape (a cottage, a striped café awning, a stadium arch, a neon marquee, a lanterned fortune tent) rather than a text label, and a real lake with a dock sits in the landscape as a genuine terrain feature rather than another building box. A handful of NPC villagers wander the town with their own faces and idle routines; walking up to one and interacting starts a short, wholesome conversation — quiet small talk with no transactional purpose. NPCs may also mention today’s island event. A corner minimap helps players orient themselves as they explore.

### 5.10 Progression
Better jobs, furniture, clothing, puppy accessories, new areas, additional mini-games, fortuneteller card variants, and richer island-event calendars unlock over time as players earn and play.

## 6. Game Goal & Themes

The overall goal is not only to adopt a puppy but to build a happy life together with that puppy, inside a small world that is pleasant simply to exist in. The experience is designed to emphasize responsibility, kindness, gentle effort, friendship, caring for animals, and cozy daily life — with a specific intent behind that tone: to give players a short, restorative pause after work or school, in a game built to be heartwarming for everyone, regardless of gender or age.

## 9. Conclusion

Paws & Pause pairs a simple, emotionally resonant goal — earning the right to bring home and care for a puppy that feels personally matched to you — with the steady, forgiving rhythms cozy-game players already love, and a clear point of view about who it's for: anyone who wants a warm, low-pressure place to spend a little time. The current build already proves out an unusually complete slice of that vision — a growing mini-game roster (starting with café, fishing, karts, and arcade as examples), a morning board with AI fortune + island events + easy world-news summaries, AI photo-match adoption, a real customization system, a talking town, and the full emotional arc from first job to first walk with a new best friend — within a course project timeline.
